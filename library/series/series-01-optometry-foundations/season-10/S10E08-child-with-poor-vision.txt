Host: Before the drops, the mother asked a question I want answered properly for everyone listening: is long sightedness the same as short sightedness, only backwards? She had always used the two words interchangeably, and she is far from alone in that.

Guide: Backwards, but with a twist, and one plain paragraph will do it. Short sightedness sees near clearly and blurs distance. Long sightedness, his story, struggles most with near, because the eye's own focusing must work extra hours to pull close things sharp; distance usually costs less. The twist is that young eyes can hide long sightedness with that muscle effort, which is why it stays invisible in children for years. Short sight cannot be hidden that way, so it tends to announce itself earlier.

Host: In plain language, here is what his eyes have been doing.
