Guide: The pupils are the dark centers of the eyes, the doorways that let light travel inward. In the preliminary stage they receive two kinds of attention. First, the optometrist simply looks. Are the two pupils similar in size, round, sitting centered? Then comes the light. A small light is brought close, and the optometrist watches the pupils respond, tightening when the light arrives and relaxing when it moves away. Why here, why now? Because that response is a fast, harmless, and remarkably informative window into the pathways connecting the eyes to the brain. A healthy response is quietly reassuring. A sluggish or uneven response is a flag worth chasing later in the visit. Notice how careful that sentence is. A flag, not a verdict. Later, not now.
